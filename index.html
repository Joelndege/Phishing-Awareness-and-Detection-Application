01 / KNOW THE THREAT

What is
phishing?

Phishing is a trick: someone impersonates a person or organisation you trust to get you to click a harmful link, open an unsafe attachment, send money, or reveal private information. It can arrive by email, text, social media, or phone.

The message may look convincing. Focus on what it asks you to do and whether you expected it.

02 / SPOT THE CLUES

Read the message.
Look for these signals.

One clue alone does not prove a message is fake. A surprising request plus pressure, a strange address, or a link you did not expect deserves a pause.

01↗

A link that feels off

Check the actual web address before opening it. Look for misspellings, extra words, unusual domains, or shortened links. A logo and display name can be copied.

secure-your-account.example  ≠  the real organisation's site
02✉

Sender mismatch

Read the full address, not just the name beside it. A small spelling change or unrelated domain can reveal an impersonator. Replies can also be spoofed.

“IT Support” <helpdesk@odd-domain.test>
03◷

Pressure or fear

“Act now,” account closure threats, surprise refunds, or too-good-to-be-true rewards push you to react before thinking. Urgency is a reason to verify.

“Final warning — verify within 10 minutes!”
04▤

Requests for secrets

Unexpected requests for passwords, one-time codes, bank details, gift cards, or payment should be checked independently. Never share a sign-in code.

“Reply with your password to unlock access.”
05⌑

Unexpected attachments

Do not open files you were not expecting, especially if the sender pressures you. Confirm with the person through a separate, known contact method.

“Invoice overdue” — but you ordered nothing
06◎

Odd context or tone

Does the greeting, request, timing, or writing style fit the sender? Good spelling does not guarantee safety; scams can be carefully written.

A familiar name with a completely new request

03 / PRACTISE THE PAUSE

Can you spot
the warning signs?

Read this fictional message. Nothing here is clickable. Pick out the clues before revealing the breakdown.

PAUSECHECKVERIFY
FICTIONAL TRAINING EXAMPLESAMPLE
FromCampus IT Help <security@campus-login-help.test>
SubjectURGENT: Your student account closes today
Hello Student,

We found a problem with your mailbox. Your account will be deleted in 30 minutes. Click below and confirm your password and one-time code to keep access.

VERIFY ACCOUNT NOW

Thank you,
Campus IT

Four things to question

  • Lookalike sender domain: “campus-login-help.test” is not established by the display name.
  • Artificial urgency: a 30-minute deadline pressures the reader.
  • Password and one-time code request: never disclose sign-in codes in response to an email.
  • Unexpected sign-in link: navigate to the known campus website yourself or contact IT using a trusted directory.

Training domains ending in .test are reserved for examples. This sample is fictional.

04 / TAKE A SAFE NEXT STEP

When unsure,
slow it down.

You do not have to solve the message on your own. Break the chain: don’t interact, verify through a trusted channel, and report it.

  1. 1

    Don’t click or reply

    Avoid links, attachments, phone numbers in the message, and “unsubscribe” links in a suspicious email.

  2. 2

    Verify independently

    Open the organisation's known app or type its familiar website address yourself. Call a number from a card or official directory.

  3. 3

    Report, then remove

    Use your email provider’s report-phishing option or notify your organisation’s IT team. Follow local reporting procedures, then delete it.

  4. 4

    If you already clicked

    Tell your IT team or service provider promptly. If you entered a password, change it on the real site and anywhere else it was reused. If you shared a code or payment details, contact the relevant provider immediately.
